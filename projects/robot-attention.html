Multi-Person Robot Attention System

Developing webcam-based human attention estimation that combines gaze estimation, object-level attention reasoning, and multi-person attention arbitration with a ROS2-integrated Gazebo simulation.

Completed summer 2026 internship · software prototype

Project status

Prototype implemented

Public overview of current scope and progress.

Research enquiries ↗

Research problem

How can a robot infer and respond to the attention of multiple people using an ordinary webcam?

Motivation

Specialized eye trackers add equipment and setup constraints. Webcam-based attention estimates offer a practical starting point for more natural human–robot interaction.

Existing approaches

The project draws on face and landmark processing, gaze estimation, and rule-based attention selection. These provide observable interaction cues, but do not directly establish what a person intends.

Research gap

I am investigating how uncertain visual cues should influence object-level attention estimates and the allocation of robot attention across multiple people.

My contribution

My internship work connects webcam-based face and gaze estimation, object-level attention reasoning, confidence-aware inference, and multi-person attention arbitration to a simulated robot response. This page describes the integrated prototype rather than claiming a new validated algorithm.

Technical methodology

Camera input is processed into face and gaze cues. Attention reasoning and arbitration select an interaction target; ROS2 communicates the target angle to a simulated eye controller. Mouth motion is used as a visual proxy for speaking activity, not microphone-based speaker identification.

Implementation

Python, OpenCV, and MediaPipe support visual processing. ROS2 Humble connects the perception and control components; a C++ Gazebo Classic plugin drives articulated eye joints and blinking eyelids.

Results

A functional software prototype connects live webcam input to simulated eye movement. Quantitative gaze accuracy, attention-selection performance, and interaction-quality results are not yet reported.

Limitations

Webcam observations are uncertain, and the simulation demonstrates a response rather than validating human interaction quality. Comparative experiments and uncertainty-aware filtering remain further work.

Future work

Compare attention policies and filtering strategies, then evaluate gaze error, target-selection accuracy, response latency, and target-switch stability. These are planned evaluation directions, not completed experiments.
